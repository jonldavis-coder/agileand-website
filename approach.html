Our approach

Small steps, real progress

Lasting change comes from many small improvements that people understand and own. Whether the change is agile ways of working or AI, our approach is built to deliver early, visible progress and to leave your organization more capable.

Jump to the AI approach
Four stages

Discover, design, enable, sustain

Discover

Conversations, observation, and a lightweight survey to understand goals, constraints, and how work flows today.

Design

Together we choose a small set of changes, the teams to start with, and the signals that will show whether it's working.

Enable

Training paired with coaching on real work. We facilitate early on, then step back as your people take the lead.

Sustain

Regular check-ins, internal champions, and simple routines that keep improvement going after the engagement ends.

AI implementation & business case realization

A DMAIC-inspired path for AI, beyond the hype

Borrowing the discipline of Six Sigma's Define, Measure, Analyze, Improve, Control cycle, we help you move from AI ideas to value you can measure and sustain, one use case at a time.

1. Define

Choose the right use case: a real problem, a named owner, and an outcome that matters to customers or the business. Agree what is in and out of scope.

2. Measure

Baseline how the work performs today, such as cycle time, cost per item, error rate, or customer effort, so improvement can be shown rather than assumed.

3. Analyze

Find root causes and check fit: is AI the right tool, is the data ready, what are the risks, and what would it take to adopt? Build the business case from evidence.

4. Improve

Run a small, time-boxed pilot with real users. Compare results to the baseline, capture what people learned, and make a clear go, adjust, or stop decision.

5. Control

Scale what works with monitoring, ownership, guardrails, and benefit tracking, so value is sustained after launch. Then reinvest in the next use case.

What “beyond the hype” means

No promised returns, no tool-first rollouts, and no skipping the baseline. If a use case doesn't show value in a pilot, stopping early is a good outcome.

Measuring progress

Signals that matter

We agree on a few simple signals up front, chosen with your teams and leaders, and revisit them regularly. Typical examples include:

  • How long work takes to go from idea to customer
  • How predictable delivery is from cycle to cycle
  • Quality signals such as rework and escaped defects
  • Customer and stakeholder feedback
  • Team health and sustainable pace
Principles in practice

What this looks like day to day

Start with a goal

Every change is linked to an outcome someone cares about, so it's easy to tell whether it's helping.

Experiment, then scale

Try changes with one or two teams, learn, and only then expand what works.

Involve the people affected

Teams help design their own ways of working, which builds understanding and ownership.

Make work visible

Shared boards and simple measures turn opinions into conversations about facts.

Inspect and adapt

Regular reviews of the change itself, not just the work, so the plan keeps improving.

Hand over early

We coach internal champions from day one so capability stays when we leave.

Let's talk about where your teams want to go

Tell us what you're working on and what's getting in the way. We'll suggest a practical next step, whether that's a workshop, coaching, or a short assessment.
