How to choose agile training that sticks

What to look for in agile training so new skills actually show up in day-to-day work.

By the Agile& team · November 1, 2026

The problem with one-off training

Many people have sat through an agile course, enjoyed it, and then gone back to work where nothing changed. Training alone rarely changes behavior. The environment, habits, and support people return to matter at least as much as the session itself.

Start with the change you want

Before choosing a course, write down what you hope will be different afterward. For example: “Our retrospectives lead to real improvements,” or “Product owners can explain why the top backlog items are on top.” Clear goals make it easier to pick the right content and audience.

A checklist for evaluating training

  • Right audience: Is it designed for the people attending, whether teams, product roles, or leaders?
  • Hands-on: Is most of the time spent practicing rather than listening?
  • Your context: Can exercises use your real work, tools, and constraints?
  • Whole team: Can people who work together learn together?
  • Follow-up: Is there coaching or a plan to apply the skills afterward?
  • Leader involvement: Will leaders understand and support the new ways of working?

After the session

Plan for the first few weeks after training. Pick one or two practices to try right away, schedule a short check-in to discuss what's working, and make sure someone is available to help with questions. Small, early wins build confidence.

How Agile& approaches training
